The programs, in detail

Four programs of 6 to 12 weeks, all taught on your own project with weekly photo feedback.

Program 01 · From nothing

Learn to knit

We start with holding the needles and end with a finished hat. Along the way you'll learn the two things beginners rarely get taught: how to fix mistakes instead of ripping everything out, and how to read your knitting so you know where you are.

  • 8 weeks
  • Complete beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • Yarn and needles

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · The basics

Cast on, knit stitch, and a tension that isn't strangling the needle.

Weeks 3–4 · Purl and patterns

Ribbing, stockinette and reading your fabric.

Weeks 5–6 · Fixing

Dropped stitches, twisted stitches, and unknitting a row.

Weeks 7–8 · A finished hat

In the round, decreases, and a tidy finish.

Weeks 1–2 · First stitches

Chain, single crochet and consistent tension.

Weeks 3–4 · Taller stitches

Double and treble, and keeping edges straight.

Weeks 5–6 · In the round

Circles that lie flat, and amigurumi shaping.

Weeks 7–8 · A project

Something finished, with the ends properly woven in.

Program 02 · Faster than it looks

Crochet basics

Crochet works up quickly and frustrates people for different reasons than knitting — usually stitch counts and knowing where to put the hook. We work on both, then a project: a blanket square, an amigurumi, or a garment if you'd rather.

  • 8 weeks
  • Beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • A hook and yarn

Start this program

Program 03 · Gauge is the whole game

Garments that fit

The reason handknit sweaters go wrong is almost always gauge and size choice. We swatch properly — washed and blocked, not a two-inch square — measure you, pick a size based on ease rather than your dress size, and adjust length and shaping as you go.

  • 12 weeks
  • Can knit basics
  • 4 hours a week
  • Sweater quantity of yarn

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · Swatch

A real swatch, washed, measured and honest.

Weeks 3–4 · Size and ease

Choosing from your measurements, not from a label.

Weeks 5–9 · The body

Knitting with checkpoints, trying it on as you go.

Weeks 10–12 · Sleeves and finishing

Getting the sleeves right and blocking properly.

Weeks 1–3 · Two colors

Holding both yarns and keeping floats loose.

Weeks 4–6 · Reading charts

Following a chart without losing your place.

Weeks 7–8 · Seaming

Mattress stitch and joins that disappear.

Weeks 9–10 · Blocking

Making the finished piece look like the pattern photo.

Program 04 · The last ten percent

Colorwork & finishing

Colorwork intimidates people because of puckering, which is a tension issue with a straightforward fix. We also spend real time on finishing — weaving in ends, seaming, picking up stitches, blocking — because that's where handmade either looks professional or doesn't.

  • 10 weeks
  • Comfortable knitting
  • 4 hours a week
  • Two or more colors
